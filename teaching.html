Teaching and Mentorship

Sociology, International Relations, Environmental Policy, and Water Resources

I have taught undergraduate and graduate students and Professional leaners in the United States and Pakistan.

Teaching Philosophy

I believe students learn best when they connect political and policy theories, analytical methods, and empirical evidence to problems in the world and to their own lived experiences. Drawing on my work as a scholar and practitioner in public policy, urban and environmental governance, spatial justice, and climate resilience, I ask students to examine not only how institutions and policies are designed, but also how political decisions shape their implementation and distribute benefits, costs, and risks across communities.

I organize my courses around three goals: building strong conceptual foundations, developing rigorous research and analytical skills, and applying knowledge to challenges facing governments, institutions, and communities. Students test ideas through simulations, such as a United Nations Security Council negotiation and a water policy simulation, and learn from guest speakers from Oklahoma, the United States, and the Global South. In 2026, I led along our chair, twelve students to the Pi Sigma Alpha National Student Research Conference, World Bank and met with the Ambassador at the Embassy of Pakistan in Washington, D.C. As a first generation scholar who learned in a second language, I make expectations clear, break major assignments into stages with feedback, and mentor students from research question to publication. My aim is to prepare thoughtful, socially responsive graduates who use evidence to evaluate political decisions and contribute to more equitable public outcomes.

Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion

As an internal migrant from South Waziristan whose native language, Pashto, differed from the Urdu and Punjabi of my classmates and teachers in Islamabad, I know how much one teacher’s support can change a student’s path. I work to create classrooms where students of every language, background, and identity are respected and able to contribute, a commitment that also guided my work with gender and religious minorities at the Youth Advocacy Council Pakistan and with the transgender community through TransHelp.
