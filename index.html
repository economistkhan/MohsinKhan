He is an Assistant Professor of Politics, Law, and Society (tenure track) at East Central University in Oklahoma, where he administers the Water Resources Policy and Management (WRPM) program. He holds a Ph.D. in Public Policy from Northeastern University (2025) and a Ph.D. in Sociology (Development Studies) from the College of Humanities and Development Studies, China Agricultural University. He is also a Development Policy Global Ambassador Scholar at the KDI School of Public Policy and Management in South Korea.

Throughout his career, he has worked with governments, universities, and nonprofit organizations, including the Mayor's Office in Boston, KOICA (Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Korea), DFID, USAID, and CIVICUS World Alliance for Citizen Participation across Asia and Africa, to implement large scale development aid projects and evaluate the impact of donor funded programs. He was listed in the 2024 Marquis Who's Who for his leadership in development cooperation and policy initiatives. His startup TransHelp was a semifinalist for MIT Solve in the 2024 Global Health Equity Challenge.

Research

His research examines how political institutions, public investment, environmental conditions, and climate change shape spatial inequality, public health, human mobility, and community resilience. Working at the intersection of political economy, development economics, environmental justice, and public health, he asks three questions: How do political and institutional decisions shape the distribution of infrastructure, public services, and environmental resources across places? How do climate hazards affect health, livelihoods, food security, and population movement differently across social groups? And how can urban governance and community participation make climate adaptation and sustainability transitions more equitable?

He addresses these questions through an interdisciplinary approach that combines political economy, geographic information systems (GIS), satellite remote sensing, spatial statistics, econometrics, machine learning, network analysis, policy evaluation, and qualitative and participatory methods. Across research in the United States, Pakistan, and other international settings, he has examined infrastructure inequality, the politics of public investment, poverty and community development, climate-induced food insecurity, flood-related agricultural damage, urban heat and maternal and child health, climate-related vulnerability and migration, environmental justice, and the implementation of emergency policies. His work also follows a participatory approach, centering community narratives and equity in planning. See Research for the full agenda and Publications for articles and work in progress.

Education and recognition

In addition to his doctoral degrees, he holds a master's degree in Development Policy (Development Economics), an MPhil in Rural Development, and a BSc (Hons) in Agriculture. He has received scholarships and awards from NUMUN Netherlands, the British Council, the Asia-Europe Foundation, the KDI School, and the Beijing Government.

At Northeastern he assisted in the Sustainability, Race, and Environmental Justice Open Classroom, and he previously served as adjunct faculty in the Department of Governance and Public Policy at NUML Islamabad. He placed in the top three at an MIT health innovation and racial justice policy hackathon (October 2021), was shortlisted for Princeton University's START Entrepreneurship Program (2023), and was named an Associate Fellow of the Commonwealth Society, an Asia-Europe Foundation fellow, and a US Emerging Leader in GreenFin24 by GreenBiz. His work has been featured by DiploFoundation, Editor Times, The Dayspring, Technology Times, KOICA, CIVICUS, and the KDI School. More in Media.
