Research

Political economy, development, environmental justice, and public health in a changing climate, across the United States, Pakistan, and beyond.

My research examines how political institutions, public investment, environmental conditions, and climate change shape spatial inequality, public health, human mobility, and community resilience. Working at the intersection of political economy, development economics, environmental justice, and public health, I ask three questions: How do political and institutional decisions shape the distribution of infrastructure, public services, and environmental resources across places? How do climate hazards affect health, livelihoods, food security, and population movement differently across social groups? And how can urban governance and community participation make climate adaptation and sustainability transitions more equitable?

I address these questions through an interdisciplinary approach that combines political economy, geographic information systems (GIS), satellite remote sensing, spatial statistics, econometrics, machine learning, network analysis, policy evaluation, and qualitative and participatory methods. Across research in the United States, Pakistan, and other international settings, I have examined infrastructure inequality, the politics of public investment, poverty and community development, climate-induced food insecurity, flood-related agricultural damage, urban heat, climate-related vulnerability and migration, environmental justice, and the implementation of emergency policies.

Urban political economy, infrastructure, and spatial inequality

My doctoral research, Spatial Equity, Climate Change, and Community Resilience (Northeastern University, 2025), established the foundation of this agenda. I developed a Spatial Representative Infrastructure Index to examine disparities in access to utilities, telecommunications, transportation, and social infrastructure across Pakistan. Using large household datasets, GIS, spatial statistics, and machine learning, I documented persistent inequalities across provinces and between urban and rural areas, showing how spatial evidence can identify underserved communities and test whether public investment reaches populations with the greatest needs. My research on Islamabad extends this work through a GIS-based analysis of park and green space accessibility, examining unequal access through the lenses of environmental and spatial justice.

I am extending these questions to the political economy of public investment. An ongoing project, “Road to Resilience,” uses Pakistan’s Annual Development Program and electoral data to investigate how political competition shapes the allocation of development funds: whether competitive constituencies receive different levels or types of investment, how these patterns vary across sectors and places, and what they mean for regional inequality. A related project analyzes the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) road network with network centrality and percolation methods to assess how major infrastructure investment redistributes regional connectivity and vulnerability. Earlier work comparing government and NGO service delivery in rural Pakistan and a coauthored evaluation of community development programs in Social Indicators Research inform this institutional focus.

At the MIT Technology and Policy Program, I codeveloped the Health Equity and Justice Dashboard with the Population Reference Bureau, using spatial and demographic data to examine disparities in health and social conditions across the United States.

Climate, environmental exposure, health, and human mobility

A second strand examines how climate hazards and environmental conditions affect livelihoods, food security, health, and population movement. Echoes of Survival (2025) examines how women, internally displaced people, smallholders, tenants, daily wage workers, and community leaders experience climate-related food insecurity and adapt to environmental disruption, showing through field research and grounded theory how environmental shocks intersect with gender, socioeconomic disadvantage, cultural expectations, and institutional constraints. With Daniel Aldrich, I extended this work quantitatively in Beyond Gender (2026), which shows how geography and institutions, including social capital, shape household food security and climate vulnerability in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

I complement this community-centered research with satellite-based environmental analysis. My study of agricultural damage from the 2022 Pakistan floods integrates multi-sensor satellite imagery, precipitation data, and geospatial analysis to map inundation and identify areas of substantial crop loss. My research on the geography of climate vulnerability combines household survey data, EM-DAT disaster records, and remote sensing to examine where climate-related hazards concentrate and what they mean for women and marginalized populations. Turning to cities, a coauthored study of land use change and urban heat islands in major Pakistani cities from 2013 to 2023 examines how urban expansion relates to rising surface heat, and a project in progress links land surface temperature exposure to maternal and child health outcomes using multilevel models.

Climate change also reshapes where people live and move. With colleagues, I codeveloped an Earth–Human Digital Twin, a physics-informed artificial intelligence framework that integrates satellite observations, WorldPop population data, socioeconomic indicators, and CMIP6 climate projections to predict climate-related human vulnerability and migration. I am extending this approach to examine how extreme heat, air pollution, floods, droughts, and wildfires intersect with population distribution, social disadvantage, and access to essential services.

Urban governance, environmental justice, and community resilience

My research also investigates how institutional decisions shape communities’ capacity to prepare for, respond to, and recover from environmental hazards. In coauthored research with Daniel Aldrich and colleagues on California’s Agricultural Pass program, we interviewed county commissioners, fire officials, farmers, and farmworkers in Napa and Sonoma Counties to examine how wildfire emergency policies affect worker safety, agricultural operations, and community resilience. The work shows that the effects of emergency policies depend not only on their formal design but on how county institutions, employers, and workers interpret and implement them.

At East Central University, my work with the Oka’ Institute and local and Tribal partners connects environmental analysis and policy research with community priorities around water resources, sustainability, and resilience to hazards such as flash flooding and tornadoes. I am also examining how cities allocate parks, recreation facilities, and social infrastructure across neighborhoods, and how these decisions shape equitable access and community resilience. In Greater Boston, I applied the Envision sustainability rating framework to the Malden River Works project in Malden, Massachusetts, examining how physical design, environmental hazards, and social outcomes interact. That work informs a related interest in the distributional consequences of urban greening, whose benefits can be uneven and can interact with rising property values, housing costs, and displacement.

Participatory and community engaged research

Much of my work begins with the people most affected by the problems I study. In Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, my fieldwork for Echoes of Survival used grounded theory to center the narratives of women, internally displaced people, smallholders, tenants, and daily wage workers, so that their own accounts of climate stress and adaptation shaped the analytical framework rather than only confirming it. Related ethnographic work examines how food consumption differs between rural and urban low income women in Pakistan, and why.

In planning research, I study equity as a question of who has access and who decides. My analysis of park and green space accessibility in Islamabad, and my work on how cities allocate parks, recreation, and social infrastructure across neighborhoods, ask whether public amenities reach residents with the greatest need, and how residents can take part in the decisions that shape their neighborhoods.

This commitment comes from practice. As cofounder and chief executive of the Youth Advocacy Council Pakistan, I led programs that created employment and leadership opportunities for gender and religious minorities, with a board that today is half minority members. As founder of TransHelp, a trans led health technology social enterprise, I work with the transgender community in Pakistan to widen access to affordable health and legal aid; TransHelp was a semifinalist in the 2024 MIT Solve Global Health Equity Challenge. These experiences shape how I design research: with communities as partners in defining questions and interpreting findings.

Development, poverty, and public health

My work in development economics measures deprivation and evaluates programs meant to reduce it. I have estimated multidimensional poverty in South-West Nigeria and Pakistan using the Alkire–Foster method, assessed the impact of climate change on dairy production with Ricardian methods, and studied the determinants of girls’ higher education and women’s socioeconomic empowerment in Gilgit-Baltistan. With Prof. Jun Borras (ISS, Netherlands), I investigated how transnational land acquisition in Pakistan affected local ecosystems, livelihoods, and displacement. My public health research includes the health repercussions of child marriage for mothers and children in the Middle East and work on HIV prevention uptake among marginalized groups in Pakistan, and my analysis of institutional failure in Burundi examines the roots of conflict beyond ethnicity.

Future directions

I will combine quantitative, qualitative, and computational approaches to examine both the distribution of inequality and the political and institutional processes that produce it. Alongside econometrics, GIS, spatial statistics, remote sensing, network analysis, and machine learning, I will use textual analysis of policy documents, legislation, development plans, and media coverage, together with interviews, focus groups, case studies, participatory research, and scenario modeling.

Political economy of urban inequality. How do political competition, local institutions, land use decisions, and public investment shape urban development? Building on the Punjab Annual Development Program data, the CPEC network, and work with cities on the allocation of public amenities, I will ask whether electoral incentives shape which neighborhoods receive infrastructure, how local governance affects access to transportation, water, sanitation, and green space, and how sustainability investments affect housing affordability and displacement, comparing cities in the United States and the Global South.

Climate, environment, and unequal health risks. I will examine how extreme heat, wildfire smoke, and air pollution interact with housing quality, energy affordability, occupation, age, income, disability, and access to public services, combining satellite land surface temperature, air quality, meteorological, demographic, and health data. I will also evaluate whether cooling infrastructure, weatherization, energy efficiency, and urban greening reduce health inequalities, which neighborhoods benefit, and whether environmental improvements contribute to displacement pressures.

Participation, governance, and decision support. Drawing on community engaged research, dashboard development, and the Earth–Human Digital Twin, I will study who participates in resilience decisions, whose priorities shape public investment, and whether adaptation reaches the communities facing the greatest risks from heat, floods, tornadoes, and wildfires. With public agencies and community partners, including local and Tribal partners in Oklahoma, I aim to build transparent, locally validated tools that identify vulnerable areas and assess the distributional consequences of policy options.
