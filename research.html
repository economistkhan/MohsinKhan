Research

Political Economy, Development, Environmental Justice, and Public Health in a Changing Climate

My research examines how political institutions, public investment, environmental conditions, and climate change shape spatial inequality, public health, human mobility, and community resilience. I combine political economy with GIS, satellite remote sensing, spatial statistics, econometrics, machine learning, network analysis, and qualitative and participatory methods.

Urban Political Economy and Spatial Inequality

My dissertation, Spatial Equity, Climate Change, and Community Resilience (Northeastern University, 2025), developed a Spatial Representative Infrastructure Index to measure unequal access to utilities, transportation, and social infrastructure across Pakistan, and a GIS analysis of park access in Islamabad. Ongoing projects ask how political competition shapes the allocation of Pakistan’s Annual Development Program funds and how the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor road network redistributes regional connectivity. At the MIT Technology and Policy Program, I codeveloped a Health Equity and Justice Dashboard with the Population Reference Bureau.

Climate, Health, and Human Mobility

Echoes of Survival (2025) and Beyond Gender (2026, with Daniel Aldrich) show how climate shocks interact with gender, poverty, and social capital to shape food insecurity in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Using satellite data, I have mapped crop damage from the 2022 Pakistan floods, studied urban heat islands in major Pakistani cities, and am linking land surface temperature to maternal and child health. With colleagues, I codeveloped an Earth–Human Digital Twin that uses satellite, population, and CMIP6 climate data to predict climate-related vulnerability and migration.

Governance and Environmental Justice

With Daniel Aldrich and colleagues, I studied California’s Agricultural Pass program, showing that the effects of wildfire emergency policies depend on how county institutions, employers, and workers implement them. At East Central University, I work with the Oka’ Institute and local and Tribal partners on water resources and resilience to flash flooding and tornadoes. In Greater Boston, I applied the Envision sustainability rating framework to the Malden River Works project, which informs my interest in who benefits from urban greening.

Participatory Research

My fieldwork centers the voices of the people most affected. The grounded theory research behind Echoes of Survival built its framework from the narratives of women, displaced people, smallholders, and daily wage workers. In planning research, I study equity as a question of who has access to public amenities and who takes part in deciding where they go. This approach grew from practice: leading community programs for gender and religious minorities at the Youth Advocacy Council Pakistan, and working with the transgender community through TransHelp.

Development and Public Health

I have measured multidimensional poverty in Nigeria and Pakistan, evaluated community development programs, and studied girls’ education and women’s empowerment in Gilgit-Baltistan, the health effects of child marriage in the Middle East, and institutional failure in Burundi.

Future Directions

Next, I plan to examine how electoral incentives and local institutions shape which neighborhoods receive infrastructure; how heat, wildfire smoke, and air pollution interact with housing and income to produce unequal health risks; and whether community participation helps adaptation investments reach the places at greatest risk, comparing cities in the United States and the Global South.
